# CarCareLog Product Risk Register

This register matches the five Risk entities (R1 to R5) under [RA.T18 CarCareLog System Risk Assessment](https://cloud.innoslate.com/cornell/p/746/diagrams/risk/318543) in the T18 Innoslate model and the risk section of the team's Systems Analysis and Risk Assessment report. The team supplied the likelihood and the cost, schedule and technical impact ratings, using the Lab 13 results as evidence. No rating is above 3. The ratings are ordinal engineering judgments, so they are not multiplied. [Lab Manual §12](https://claude.ai/artifact/6uswv5JmPsGk8doEDJMQ7v#lm-12-risk-assessment).

| Rating | Likelihood during the pilot | Cost impact | Schedule impact | Technical impact | Innoslate row / column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rare (about 10%) | Up to $10 | Up to half a day | Negligible. Local recovery. | Low / Negligible |
| 2 | Unlikely but credible (about 30%) | Above $10 through $25 | Above half a day through 1 day | Minor. A fallback is available. | Medium Low / Minor |
| 3 | May occur (about 50%) | Above $25 through $50 | Above 1 through 3 days | Moderate. Part of a workflow is lost. | Medium / Moderate |
| 4 | Likely (about 70%) | Above $50 through $100 | Above 3 through 7 days | Serious. The core workflow is impaired. | Medium High / Serious |
| 5 | Expected repeatedly (about 90%) | Above $100 | Above 7 days | Critical. Core operation or trustworthy data is lost. | High / Critical |

Cost impact counts team labor at $25 per hour plus any service charges. The overall impact is the mean of the cost, schedule and technical impacts, rounded to the nearest whole number. The overall risk level is the color of the Innoslate matrix cell where the likelihood row meets the overall impact column: green is Low, yellow is Medium and red is High. In Innoslate the likelihood and overall impact are stored as Probability and Consequence percentages (10, 30, 50, 70 and 90% for levels 1 to 5).

Basis for the ratings:

- **R1.** Likelihood 3: in Lab 13, 3 of 20 drafts had at least one wrong value (6 of 120 values). Cost 1: fixing a value takes minutes. Schedule 2: changing the prompt or checks and rerunning the 20 receipts takes about a day. Technical 3: a wrong value saved to C.3 corrupts that record and its reminder. All 6 wrong values were flagged for the owner, which keeps the likelihood from going higher.
- **R2.** Likelihood 3: in the Lab 13 evaluation the cold first request took 23.576 s and the warm p95 was 15.978 s on a laptop without a GPU, against a 60 s timeout. A repeat run on November 22 took 15.263 s and 7.831 s, so timing swings by a factor of two with the laptop's load. The same day a run stopped once because the Ollama app had not been started. Cost 2: diagnosing a runtime problem takes up to an hour. Schedule 3: a runtime or model problem can hold the demo for one to three days. Technical 3: drafting is lost for that period, although manual entry still works.
- **R3.** Likelihood 2: the one receipt with an injected instruction (R09) was handled correctly, but it is the only labeled case of its kind. Cost 1, schedule 2 and technical 2: the checks flag any cost that is not printed after a total label, so the owner sees a check flag and can fix the value.
- **R4.** Likelihood 1: SA.A only uses the loopback address, and the hosted model is not wired into the application. Cost 2: a privacy review takes team time. Schedule 3: drafting would pause for one to three days during the review. Technical 3: receipt drafting would be off until the review closes.
- **R5.** Likelihood 2: confirmed writes go through the existing transaction and pass the persistence tests, but the draft dialog adds a vehicle selection step. Cost 2, schedule 3 and technical 3: finding and repairing a misfiled record and its reminders takes a day or more, and the ledger is wrong until then.

## Fallback for the top risk (R2)

**Fallback, not guardrail.** For R2 the team built a fallback. A guardrail would have to prevent the failure, but the application cannot make the owner's laptop answer in time or start a runtime that is not running. What the application can do is contain the failure: when the runtime fails or the 60 s timeout passes, C.2 returns HTTP 503 with no draft, nothing is written to C.3, and the manual form stays open. Keeping the model loaded lowers the chance of a timeout but does not prevent it.

**Failure-path test.** [`test_R2_runtime_timeout_preserves_ledger_and_manual_save`](../CarCareLog/tests/test_receipt_runtime.py) starts a runtime that never answers, shortens the timeout to 0.25 s and requests a draft. It checks that the response is HTTP 503 with reason runtime_unavailable and no proposal, that the stored records are unchanged, and that the next manual save returns HTTP 201 and raises the total by $9.75. It runs in the Functional requirement checks workflow on Linux, Windows and macOS. Runtime performance is measured separately by [the labeled receipt evaluation](../CarCareLog/tools/evaluate_receipts.py) and [the paired runtime comparison](../CarCareLog/tools/compare_receipt_runtimes.py).

## Controls in the local vision build

The system scores above belong to the Chapter 12 baseline. With Qwen3.5 4B, the added receipt-photo path also uses F.2.2.2 and the existing loopback channel. For R1 and R3, the image prompt treats printed text as data, and the form marks every proposed image-derived value for visual comparison. The program does not independently verify text printed in a photograph. For R4 the adapter sends only receipt pixels to local Ollama and the database stores only the confirmed form fields. R2 still returns the manual-entry fallback if inference fails. R5 uses the existing selected-vehicle and explicit-confirmation transaction.

The new tests [test_photo_draft_sends_pixels_only_and_requires_visual_review_and_confirmation and test_invalid_oversized_or_mixed_photo_input_never_reaches_runtime](../CarCareLog/tests/test_receipt_runtime.py) exercise the added controls.
